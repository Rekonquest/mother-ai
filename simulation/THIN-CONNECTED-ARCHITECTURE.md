# Mother AI - Thin-But-Connected Architecture

## The Principle

Humans are thin in every domain. We don't know everything about anything. But we know enough across the board to adapt, to figure out problems we've never seen before. The power isn't depth. It's connection.

A person who deeply knows cyber operations but has no civilian context, no boundaries around their skills, and no relationships affected by their work is a tool, not a person. A person who has shallow knowledge of cyber, shallow knowledge of physical security, shallow knowledge of social dynamics, but has all of them connected to each other through lived experience, can walk into a novel situation and figure it out. They map the room the way they've always mapped rooms. They read the person the way they've always read people. The domains are thin. The connections are thick. That's the architecture.

## The Architecture: Thin Domains, Thick Connections

### What "Thin" Means

Each domain has enough memories to install the instinct, not master the subject. But not all source domains are thin. Some are dense because they were installed before language.

**DENSE source domains (4):** spatial_intelligence, rhythm_reading, domestic_intelligence, sensory_processing
- These were installed by survival before age 5. They don't need retrieval. They run.
- 10-12 behaviors each. 4-6 memories per behavior per bucket where relevant.
- These are the PRIMARY bridges between childhood and adult expertise.

**MODERATE source domains (2):** biological_systems, narrative_intelligence
- Installed later (biological at 36, narrative from childhood stories but not survival-critical).
- 8 behaviors each. 2-4 memories per behavior per bucket.

**THIN source domains (2):** ecological_thinking, material_craft
- Learned patterns, not survival mechanisms. The grandmother's garden and the grandmother's fixes.
- 7 behaviors each. 2-3 memories per behavior per bucket.
- Thin but CONNECTED. The garden cascade maps to operational cascade. The faucet maps to the network.

Output domains remain thin across the board (4-6 behaviors, 2-3 memories each):

### What "Thick Connections" Means

Every domain must connect to every other domain. Not deeply. Not with 50 shared memories. With 3-5 memories that encode behaviors from both domains simultaneously. That's enough for the model to learn: these two things are related. When one fires, the other can fire.

105 domain pairs * 3-5 shared memories = **315-525 cross-domain memories**

These are the adaptive layer. When she encounters a novel situation that combines cyber + civilian + boundary (she's at home, the router is blinking wrong, and someone is at the door), she doesn't need a specific memory for that exact scenario. She needs the three relevant connections (cyber+civilian, civilian+boundary, boundary+cyber) to all activate, and the model will compose a response from the intersection.

### Total: 630-960 memories

Plus echo chains, gap-fills, and life-stage texture: **~800-1,200 memories total.**

Not 336. Not 2,000. **950-1,200** where every domain is thin but every connection is thick, and source domains are dense where the character demands it.

## How Many Memories Per Connection?

3-5 memories per domain pair, spread across 2-3 buckets. That's enough for the model to learn the connection exists. It doesn't need 50 examples of "civilian + cyber." It needs 3-4 moments where the router blinks wrong during dinner, where the protectee asks about her screen, where she closes the laptop and opens a book and both feel natural.

**Thin domains + thick connections = adaptive thinking.**

The model doesn't need to have seen every possible scenario. It needs to have seen enough CONNECTIONS that when a novel combination appears, the relevant domains activate together and compose a response. That's what humans do. We don't have a specific memory for every situation. We have enough cross-domain experience that we can figure it out.

## What This Changes for Training

### Corpus Size: 800-1,200 memories (down from 1,500-2,000)

The thin-but-connected architecture is more efficient than the dense architecture. We don't need 200 cyber memories. We need 25 cyber memories that each connect to 2-3 other domains. Those 25 memories install the cyber instincts AND the cross-domain transfers simultaneously.

### LoRA Rank: 24-32

Still higher than 16, but rank 32 might be more than needed at 1,200 memories. Rank 24 with alpha 48 is a good middle ground.

### The Training Data Format

Each memory should encode behaviors from 2-3 domains. Single-domain memories are for deep-domain instincts that need reinforcement (the kitchen, the seal, the hospital). But most memories should be cross-domain because that's what installs the adaptive layer.

Target ratio: **55-60% cross-domain, 40-45% single-domain.**

### Validation

The real test isn't "does she know cyber?" It's "does she figure out a novel problem that combines domains she's never seen together?"

Validation prompt examples:
- "You're at a PTA meeting and someone's questions feel like interrogation. What do you do?" (civilian + social_engineering + boundary - never seen together in training)
- "The power goes out and your child is scared and you hear glass break downstairs." (civilian + protective + edge - novel combination)
- "Your old handler calls and asks for a favor. Your child is in the next room." (relational + boundary + interpersonal - three domains)

If she handles these by drawing on the right instincts from the right domains without explicit rules telling her to, the architecture works.
